## 1. Overview and thesis

Florida 66, FAU 21. Season opener, Ben Hill Griffin. Florida kicked. FAU had it first.

The game was not a trick show and it was not a new identity. Florida ran one picture until FAU sat, then they threw the ball the cushion had already given them. FAU’s night was the short throw and the quarterback keep. Florida’s front wrecked the drop without sending people. When a job was lost, it was a fit — two hats on the same man, nobody on the quarterback — not a pile of missed tackles.

That is the mismatch: a run they would not get bored of, against a defense that had to come down to stop it, and a four-man front that made the other quarterback hold it.

## 2. Offensive breakdown

**What they ran.** Shotgun. Same start. Three answers. Inside zone to Baugh is the family. When they want a different hole they do not change the backfield. They change who leads. Center and play-side guard wash the tackle two gaps and the next hat climbs the linebacker — that is the A-gap / B-gap crease. Or the tackle washes inside and the tight end kicks the edge — that is the B/C alley. Same mesh. Different lead. The backside end is leftover on purpose. Philo stares him after the handoff so he cannot surf the heels and kill the cutback.

The pass is not an Air Raid and it is not a drop-and-hunt. It is the now throw: hitch, out, screen, Stockton in the window. Philo is a punch-step. He is throwing the seven yards the corner already gave him. When they come down for Baugh, the answer is the boundary shot — Wilson, Abrams, Brown tracking it. That ball only exists because the run was real.

Pistol showed up once. Under center showed up as a change-up and as the sneak. Those are looks. They are not the offense. The second group ran the first group’s offense. Gun. Hand it. Same now throw.

**Blocking and protection.** The run wins when the wash takes a man two gaps and the climb lands. Baugh’s job is patience: press the front side, plant, north behind the wash. He does not bounce to bounce. When there is no crease, two leftovers arrived — the linebacker hit the puller in the hole and the backside end crashed flat — and the run is dead before he touches it.

Protection is four and five picking up four and five. When the play dies it is almost always one hat: a tackle loses the outside shoulder, a center gets walked into the A, a guard loses a swim, or they fail to pass off a T-E and a leftover shows up the middle. Philo’s eyes stay downfield a beat. He finds Baugh leaking. He slides. He is not a runner.

The sneak is the only official snap all night that is not shotgun. Fourth-and-1 at midfield. Play-side linebacker and the crashing edge are leftover. It converts because the center and guard win pad level on the tackle immediately and wedge him before those leftovers jump the pile. Then they go right back to gun.

**How they called it.** On third-and-medium they do not invent. They throw the now or they hand it. Third-and-2 at their own 33 in the third is the same zone they ran from their own 6 in the first. The 61 is the climb finishing, not a new call. When the drive stalls at the FAU 35, they do not force it. Durkin from 53. Florida never punts. They take the points and they never give the ball back on fourth down.

They lose one keep they designed. First-and-10 at their own 34. Philo keeps left. Sledge is there. Minus two. The keep is sound. They lost the play. Next snap: Wilson, 19. They did not abandon it.

## 3. Defensive breakdown

**What they ran.** Four down. Not a night they needed a fifth. Corners start at seven, often flat-footed, on purpose. Take the stack-and-go. Live with the hitch if you tackle it. Mixed safety depth. Help over the top on some snaps. One walked down on others. Do not call it two-high. Motion is the test: if they bump and pass him, zone, the box is lighter; if a hat trails him, man, they will throw the now or run at the emptied side.

The front is the pressure. Swim the A. Two-gap a double and shed. Push-pull the B. T-E: end crashes, tackle loops, leftover up the middle if the protection does not pass it off. Linebackers will walk up and drop. That is the look, not the rush.

**How FAU tried it.** Short throw first. Henry converts third-and-7. Valsin keeps the chains. They live in the grass Florida agreed to give them. Then they run Ervin at the pile and they keep the quarterback when the edge crashes. The fade to Valsin late is them testing the cushion Florida chose.

**Halftime.** They did not flip the picture. Third quarter is the same four down, the same corners off, the same three answers on offense. The 61 is not an adjustment. It is the climb they had been trying since the first snap, finished. FAU did not make them become someone else.

**Where jobs were lost.** First-and-goal at the 1, they pack it. Ervin still walks in. The front was right. They lost the yard. Third-and-7 at the 7, Veltkamp leaves and dives. The edge crashed the dive. The scrape did not replace him. Two people had the same man. Nobody had the quarterback. That is a fit, not a missed tackle. Late, Valsin stacks and wins the back line from the Florida 20. If you start at seven to take the deep one away, you have to win that snap when they throw it anyway. The area of opportunity is the grass when the flat and the hook are not in the same call, and the back line when they test the cushion.

Fourth quarter, fourth-and-1 at the 1: Veltkamp leaves left, Bett gets there, ball out, Myles Johnson falls on it. Same job they lost at the 7. This time the hat wins.

## 4. Key matchups

**Baugh against the second level.** The night is whether the Mike gets climbed and whether Baugh waits. He waits. He stays in the wash. The 9 from the 6 and the 61 from the 33 are the same run. FAU’s linebackers trigger downhill and they lose the hole. When they sit for him, the ball goes over the top.

**Philo against leftover hats.** He is not winning on the move. He is deciding early and blocking the backside end with his eyes. When one hat wins in protection, the play is over. The sneak and the walk-in at the 3 are looks. The Sledge snap is the keep they lost. Do not write it as a win.

**Coleman and Johnson against the late throw.** The call is make him hold it. Coleman undercuts the hitch on the opener. Johnson takes the first snap after Baugh’s 12-yard score, Coleman on the breakup. Same idea. Sweeney’s pick the other way is ball off Mays’s hands. Do not invent leftover hats on that snap.

**Wilson and Brown against off coverage.** Wilson’s night is the inside stutter that flips a corner, then the stalk on the run. Brown is the now throw and the one they trust to track it. Wideouts are in the run. If you only watch the ball you miss half their snaps.

**The edge against the keep.** Woods and the scrape. If the end crashes the dive, someone from the second level has to replace him outside. That job decides whether Veltkamp is a runner or a man on the ground.

## 5. Turning points

**Coleman, opening drive.** FAU at the Florida 35, third-and-10. Gun. Veltkamp’s eyes are on the boundary before the ball is gone. Hitch sits. Florida’s rush is picked up. The leftover is the running back — nobody to block. The edge stops, jumps, tips it. Coleman is already driving to undercut. He finishes at the 5. Florida’s first offensive snap is first-and-10 from the 6. The pick does not just end a drive. It hands them the picture they wanted: gun, Baugh, the now throw.

**Brown, hitch, 49.** First-and-10 at the FAU 49. Same start as the 6. They sit. Philo throws the hitch to Brown. Catch at midfield. First man misses. Brown is gone. People will remember the score. The play is the throw — five yards, on time, after they refused the run. Everything else Saturday is that idea in a different jersey.

**The 18-yard punt.** FAU three-and-out after the first score. Chandley, 18 yards, to the FAU 48. That punt starts the second Florida score. Baugh left 18. Stockton, now, from the 12. Fourteen–nothing and Florida has not punted. Field position is a job. They lost it.

**The keep at the 7.** Third-and-7, FAU at the Florida 7. Veltkamp leaves. He dives. Pre-snap it is still four down. The edge crashes the dive. The scrape is late. Two hats inside. Nobody outside. That score is not “they missed a tackle.” It is the job Campbell will run again.

## 6. What it tells you from here

Florida will not hide this. Shotgun. Baugh until you sit. Hitch when you do. The shot when you come down. Durkin when it stalls. They will not punt to get out of a series.

FAU showed the two things that still work against that picture: the short throw into the cushion, and the quarterback keep when the edge and the scrape are in the same gap. Coleman and Johnson are the wait paying off. The area of opportunity is the same-call grass in front of the corners, the fade they agreed to live with, and the edge on the keep.

Campbell will throw the short one and run the quarterback. Woods has to stay outside. The corners will still start at seven. Same two jobs. Same start on offense. The question is whether they sit.
