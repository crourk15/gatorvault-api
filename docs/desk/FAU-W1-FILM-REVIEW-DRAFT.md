## Buster Faulkner — Offense

**The run.** Inside zone from the gun. Most of the night there is no puller. The line steps play-side. Baugh presses the first crease, stays in the wash, pads down, and finishes through the body. He does not bounce just to bounce. When the front-side dies he plants and goes north behind the wash — that cutback is a read. We sat that footwork.

They do not change the backfield to get a different hole. They change the lead. Motion walks across and holds the second level. A tight end attaches or wings and either seals or sifts to the backside (split-zone look). On one family of snaps a guard pulls and Baugh follows him — down blocks, pull, lead. Same start. Different man in the hole.

Two crease constructions we actually sat:

1. Center and play-side guard take the tackle across two gaps. The next lineman climbs the linebacker. If that climb lands, the second level is late and Baugh is already vertical.
2. Tackle washes the defender inside. Tight end kicks the edge outside. That is an alley outside the tackle, not the A-gap. The guard climbs.

The backside end is often left unblocked. After the mesh Philo fakes the boot and stares that end so he cannot chase the cutback down the line. We sat that on the zone reps. When the tight end already kicked that end, Philo does not have to hold him.

When the run dies on the tape: the play-side linebacker hits a puller in the hole, or the backside end crashes flat, or the front wins the line and there is no second answer on the snap.

**The now throw.** Philo is catch-and-throw. Flat, out, slant, shallow, screen, in-breaker. He is not dropping to hunt a second answer on those snaps. Brown is the one they throw it to now — quick out, slant, screen. Wilson is the screen catch-and-go and the first block on the screen when Brown is the ball. Baugh chips, then leaks; when the pocket dies Philo looks for him. That flush-to-Baugh is survival, not the call.

**The boundary ball.** Clean pocket. Set platform. Wilson tracks it and adjusts. Brown tracks it. They went and got that ball more than once. If the edges collapse or an interior stunt wins the A, the shot is dead. Philo steps up or leaves. He slides. He is not a runner.

**Play-action.** Same run fake, different answer. Philo rolls and throws the flat. That leave is designed. When the pocket dies and he runs, that is not designed.

**Looks, not the offense.** Pistol showed up once — handoff to Baugh, then they went back to gun. Under center showed up as a change-up (toss, downhill, boot) and as the sneak. The sneak: center and guard win pad level on the tackle immediately. The play-side linebacker and the crashing edge are not blocked. It converts because the wedge is there before those two can jump the pile. Then they go right back to gun.

**Empty** showed up as a change-up (Baugh walks back in; FAU also emptied). Do not make it the offense.

**The second group** ran the first group’s offense. Gun. Inside zone. Same now throw. Same boundary shot when the pocket was there. Receivers still stalk.

## Brad White — Defense

**The front.** Four down. They do not need to send people to wreck a drop. What we sat: an interior swim across the center’s face that takes the A; a two-gap on the guard, shed, tackle for loss; tackles upfield caving the mesh; an edge under the tackle; interior twist; T-E (end crashes, tackle loops) that leaves a free rusher up the middle when the protection does not pass it off. Linebackers walk up and drop — that is the look, not the rush. They will send an extra on a passing snap. That is the exception. The base is four.

**Opening series, FAU.** Stretch/zone to the boundary — swim, A is gone, play dies. Inside zone the other way — two-gap, shed, loss. Third-and-long: Veltkamp stares the boundary, the hitch sits, the edge jumps and tips it, Coleman is already undercutting. Protection picked up the rush. The running back had nobody to block. The pick is eyes plus a hat in the lane. It is not a blitz win.

**Coverage.** Corners start off. Soft. They come down and wrap the out, the flat, the screen. That is a choice: take away the stack-and-go, live with the stop if you tackle it. On one snap the corner tightened, ran with the vertical, and found the ball. Safety depth is mixed. Some slices help is over the top. Some slices one walks down to fill. Do not lock two-high.

Motion tells you the coverage. If they trail the walker, it is man. If they bump and pass him, it is zone. We sat the trail.

**The keep.** The job is stay outside. When they win it, they string the keep and the edge stays square. When they lose it, the edge crashes the fake or gets sealed and nobody replaces him outside — the quarterback has the edge. We sat FAU’s boot: play-side edge bit the run fake, the scrape to replace him was late, the quarterback had a platform to the sideline. Late, backups, same rule: edge gets sealed, they chase him out. That is a fit, not a missed-tackle story.

**FAU’s menu on the tape.** Gun. Inside run. Quick to the flat and the sideline. Screen. One empty change-up. They will try the boundary ball if you sit. Florida’s answer is the front in the backfield and the secondary coming down on the short one. The grass they give is the middle — a crosser/dig found a soft spot — and the late scramble throw.

**Backups** did not change the call. Four down. Screen. Run fit. Front in the mesh. Same keep job.

## Special Teams

Florida kicked the game off. Extra points we sat were clean. Kickoff lanes stayed and they squeezed the return. Field goals we sat were snap-hold-kick; one FAU try, Florida got a hat on the ball. FAU punts: Brown fields and wants the edge, or he fair-catches. This broadcast does not show a Florida punt unit. Kneel to end it.

## What the tape says they are

**Baugh** — one-cut, low pads, contact balance. He waits for the lead. He will take the ugly interior. The offense is built for him.

**Philo** — platform, on time. First answer. He needs the pocket. Free rusher or the A-gap stunt ends the play. He is not extending with his legs.

**Wilson** — tracks the boundary ball. Stalks on the run. First block on the screen.

**Brown** — the now throw and the track. Slant, out, screen, deep.

**The front** — first step and the double. That is why they can play corners off.

**The back end** — off coverage by choice. Mixed help. The throws that hurt are the short one they agreed to live with when they do not come down together, the middle window, and the keep when the edge leaves.
