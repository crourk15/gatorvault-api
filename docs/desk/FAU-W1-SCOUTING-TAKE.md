# FAU Week 1 — scouting take (desk)

Sat the broadcast. This is the layer a fan does not see. Not a live Review.

Jerseys locked: Philo 12, Baugh 13, Brown III 1, Wilson 6. Do not invent the rest. Never Lagway.

Florida kicked off. FAU had it first. Coleman’s pick is the first live snap that matters.

---

## Why Baugh has a crease

The run is not “give it to 13.” The crease is built. Two constructions showed up.

**Wash and climb.** Center and play-side guard take the tackle *two gaps*. They are not moving him a yard. They are taking him out of the hole Baugh wants. The next hat climbs the Mike. If that climb lands, the second level is late and Baugh is already north.

**Split the edge.** Tackle washes the tackle inside. Tight end kicks the edge outside. That is a B/C alley, not an A-gap. Guard climbs. Same start. Different hole because they changed who led, not because they changed the backfield.

When they want the bounce, they still do not change the start. Tight end downs or hooks the edge. A guard pulls and logs the force. Motion walks a hat out of the fit so the overhang has to honor the flat. Baugh’s job on those is patience — press the boundary long enough for the log to land, then stick the outside foot and go north.

The backside edge is left on purpose. That is the leftover. Philo’s boot after the mesh is not decoration. He stares that hat so the end cannot surf the heels and kill the cutback. When the tight end already kicked that edge, Philo does not need to hold him. When Philo does not sell it, that hat is in the mesh.

Baugh’s cutback is the same rule every time you actually watch his feet: press the front side, plant, north behind the wash. He stays in the wash. He does not bounce to bounce. The cutback is a read, not a habit.

When there is no crease: the play-side linebacker triggered downhill and hit the puller in the hole, and the backside end crashed flat. Two leftovers arrived. That is a lost run before Baugh touches it.

## What Philo is actually doing

He is a first-look quarterback. The ball comes out because he already owned the picture.

On the now throw: he is not dropping. One punch-step, cleats in the ground, ball gone. He is throwing the cushion the corner already gave him. If the corner is at seven and flat-footed, that throw is decided before the snap.

On the boundary shot: he uses the mesh to stare the hash and freeze the middle safety. Back foot hits. Hips snap. The ball is in the air before Wilson has stacked. That is anticipation, not a jump ball. Wilson’s job on that snap is the inside stutter that flips the corner’s hips, then stack.

When the play dies: it is almost always one hat. Tackle loses the outside shoulder, or the center gets walked into the A, or a guard loses a swim. Philo’s eyes stay downfield a beat, then he finds Baugh leaking. He is not a runner. The scramble is leftover time. He slides.

He will reverse-pivot after the mesh and stare the unblocked edge. That is how he holds the leftover on the run. If you only watch the back, you miss that the quarterback is blocking with his eyes.

The sneak is a look. Fourth-and-1, under center. Play-side linebacker and the crashing edge are leftover — nobody blocks them. It converts because the center and guard win pad level on the tackle *now* and wedge him before those leftovers can jump the pile. Then they go right back to gun. Do not make the sneak the offense.

## The motion is a test

A receiver walks. You are not watching the receiver. You are watching the second level.

If they bump and pass him — zone. The box is lighter than it looks. The wash has a chance.
If a hat runs with him — man. The crease is different. They will throw the now or they will run at the emptied side.

Orbit / jet is the same test with a hold: it freezes a linebacker’s feet so the climb can finish. On the touchdown crease, motion walked the overhang out of the fit. That is why there was no leftover in the alley — they removed him before the snap.

## How the front wins

They do not need a blitz to wreck a drop.

What actually shows up:
- A tackle two-gaps a double and the Mike stays clean to fill.
- A tackle swims the guard and the A is gone on the first step.
- An edge push-pulls inside the tackle and the B-gap flashes.
- A T-E stunt: end crashes the B, tackle loops. The protection has to pass it off. When they do not, there is a leftover hat up the middle and the ball is out or it is a throwaway.

Opening drive is the whole front in three snaps. First down: swim across the center’s face, A is gone, stretch dies. Second down: two-gap the guard, shed, tackle for loss. Third-and-10 from the Florida 5: they can send a fifth and the line can still pick it up five-on-five. The leftover on that snap was the running back — nobody to block.

The leftover on the *run* is usually a safety walked down on the boundary, or the backside edge who is told to stay. When that edge stays square, the keep is dead. When he crashes the dive, someone from the second level has to replace him outside. That replacement is the whole game on the keep.

## Why the short throw is there

The corners start at seven, often flat-footed. That is a choice. They are not guessing. They are taking away the stack-and-go and living with the stop / the out / the screen.

The problem is not the cushion. The problem is when two hats are late to the same throw — the flat defender and the hook are not in the same call. One sits, one bails, the area between them is grass. That is the area of opportunity. It is not missed tackles. It is two people who did not get the same call.

On the screen: the nickel is the force (inside-out). The corner is supposed to stay the contain. When the corner comes down and the nickel is late, or the other way around, the first hat from the offense wins.

## The two picks

**Coleman, opening drive.** Veltkamp stared the boundary the moment the ball moved. The hitch sat. Coleman was already driving to undercut it. The edge stopped the rush, jumped, and got a hand on it. The tip killed the velocity. Coleman finishes a ball that was already his picture — the flutter just made it easier. Protection held. The leftover was the back. This is not a blitz win. This is eyes plus a hat in the lane.

**Sweeney, two-minute.** Official is Philo to Mays, ball off the hands, Sweeney at the FAU 30. That is a contested middle throw into a defender who was already sitting. Do not write it as a guess. Do not write it as “bad throw” without the route. The ball arrived where a sitting defender could finish because Mays did not secure it. I do not have a clean leftover-hat sit on that exact snap yet — do not invent one.

## The keep

This is the one that is not obvious from the stands.

If the edge crashes the back, the scrape has to replace him. On the snaps they lost the edge, the end went inside with the dive and the linebacker / nickel got washed inside with him. Nobody was left outside. That is not “they missed a tackle.” That is a fit. Two people had the same man. Nobody had the quarterback.

When they win it: the backside edge stays home, shoulders square, and the Mike is clean.

FAU’s boot in the middle of the night is the same rule the other way: play-side edge bit the run fake, the scrape to replace him was late, the quarterback had a platform to the sideline. Same missed job. Different jersey.

---

## The receivers’ real job

Wilson’s night is as much stalk as it is catch. He is the first hat on the screen and the seal on the bounce. The inside stutter that flips a corner is why the boundary ball is there.

Brown is the one they trust to take the now throw and the one they trust to track it. Different jobs. Same player.

The wideouts are in the run. If you only watch the ball, you miss half their snaps.

---

## Who they are (from the tape, not the card)

**Baugh** — patience, then violence. He waits for the log. He will take the ugly A-gap. He wins because the line moved a man two gaps, not because he made a man miss in space.

**Philo** — first look, punch-step, eyes that hold a leftover. He is not extending plays. He is deciding them early. When one hat wins, he is done.

**The front** — they win first step and they win the double. That is why they can play the coverage they play.

**The back end** — the call is take the deep one away. Mixed safety depth. Help over the top on some, one walked down on others. Do not call it two-high. The area of opportunity is the grass in front of them when two people are not in the same call, and the edge when the quarterback keeps it.
