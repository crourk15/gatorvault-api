# Home Was the Easy Part

By GatorVault Staff · Sep 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Florida is 2-0. That is what you are supposed to be after Florida Atlantic and Campbell. Both were at home. Neither game got close. That is why the record looks like a start. It is also why the record is not a test. Home was the easy part. Saturday at Auburn is the first night Jon Sumrall's standard has to leave the Swamp.

The first Saturday made that easy to believe. Florida Atlantic left with 66-21 and no claim on the night. Aaron Philo went 16-of-21 for 275 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception. Jadan Baugh ran 14 times for 160 yards and three scores. Vernell Brown III caught six for 117 and a touchdown. Eric Singleton Jr. hurt his ankle and did not finish. A home opener that never gets close is a gift. It is also a disguise.

Sumrall treated it like one. On the Monday after FAU, he was already watching the snaps that would not survive a real opponent. Defense was football IQ: knowing what the play is asking, the down and distance, when they speed it up, when to crowd a receiver and when to give him space. On 3rd-and-short a corner sat nine yards off the ball. That is too far. They took away the long passes and let FAU speed the game up. The interception was a catchable ball, not on Philo. Philo changed the play at the line when he needed to, and led. The win was real. The tape was the first crack of concern: the things that can lose you a Saturday when somebody can punch back.

Campbell was the next home night, and the first series let that concern out in public. Completions down the field. A field goal. Sumrall told the defensive backs not to get scared after a catch. At corner you are going to get beat. Next play. If that start shows up at Auburn while the game is still a game, the easy part is already over.

The rest of the half put it back to sleep. Philo scored from the 3. Patrick Durkin hit a 28-yard field goal. After one it was 10-3, and then Vernell Brown III scored twice on punt returns, 64 and 70, caught one from 14, and Dallas Wilson took a 40-yard touchdown. Baugh finished it from the 2 with 20 seconds left. Halftime was 45-3. Philo threw for 242 yards and two scores. Same clean quarterback, different week. Same easy night.

The second half is where the easy night started lying. Sumrall put the backups in and the flags came with them. T.J. Shanahan Jr. on back-to-back snaps, a hold that wiped a Baugh run that was already a big gain, then a personal foul. Roderick Kearney picked up another. That is 1st-and-25, and in this league the drive is already over. Seven flags after halftime. One in the first half. Three in the whole opener. Up 45-3 those flags do not change the score. If they show up when the game is still close, they are how you lose. That is not Campbell's problem anymore. That is the first thing about this team Auburn can use.

Late in the third, after the argument was already made, Baugh went 75. Last year his longest was 26 yards. In two weeks he has 296 yards and five scores on 14 carries each Saturday. Campbell stayed on three. The Swamp left with 52-3 and a 2-0 start it had not had in five years. Another home night that never got close. Another Saturday that could not test them.

The building felt like 2-0. Sumrall did not. He walked to the locker room unhappy on a night most first-year coaches would have taken, and that uneasiness is the part of the week that still matters. Happy they got the win. Not pleased with a lot of the rest. Second-team and third-team guys got a chance to prove people right or wrong, and some of them showed why they are not playing. Sumrall called the depth behind Baugh subpar. A guy went the wrong way on one run. Somebody missed a protection call. The starting offensive line was not awful and is not where it needs to be. They did not give Baugh as many rushing lanes as they should have. Year One under Sumrall is not a celebration of 52-3. It is a coach deciding, in public, that the score is not the standard. Freshmen have been here since January. Play that second half in conference and they are about to have a real rude awakening. Walk in like that and they will get absolutely embarrassed. He is not sour after a blowout. He is a first-year coach who can already see the first Saturday that can embarrass him.

Monday, that feeling was still in the building. The uneasiness had cooled. It had not left. When they rolled the depth of the roster into the game, they did not play clean football. Sumrall wants them to finish on the quarterback. He wants sacks. These games count twice, he said. Overall record and conference record. The last two weeks counted once. If he has to get anybody ready to play SEC football, they have the wrong people here. That is urgency. Auburn is where it stops being a speech.

The first measurement is the run. The spacing and the shots get everybody's attention. What kills you is the run game. If you do not stop the run against them, you have no chance. Jeremiah Cobb and Omar Mabson II are both over 5.5 yards a carry and have 265 combined. Byrum Brown ran for a thousand yards and 14 scores last year. He is physical, playing with confidence, a lot of time in this system. Home let Florida choose the terms. Saturday will not.

They may also walk in short. Eric Singleton Jr. and Emmanuel Oyebadejo are out of the boot. Both were very limited Monday. Both are questionable. Sumrall said there is a shot. Sumrall is not ready to say they will play.

The one thing that has been easy to trust is Philo. He is confident and not easily rattled. If he plays like he has played through the first part of this year, Sumrall said, he will give them a chance to win every week. At home that has been enough. Saturday it has to be enough with a crowd that is not theirs. That is the first time the easy part gets taken away from him.

Tuesday, the coordinators had to say how a standard travels. The first SEC road night is not a head-coach problem. It is an operation problem. Buster Faulkner has to take a quarterback who has only started at home and keep him clean in a building that will not help him. DJ Durkin's defense plays with its hair on fire, plays fast, plays confident. Same preparation every week, no matter who they play. You are not just playing 11 defenders. You have the crowd. Faulkner said Philo is cool, calm, and collected. They will coach him through it.

Brad White has the other half of the same test. He has to take a defense that just spent two weeks in the Swamp and put it in a fight that already knows its own offense. Auburn has been in Alex Golesh's system for years. Pace, tempo, skill, a nasty offensive line. Fist fight. Rock fight. Be ready for that. That is what the first real test of the standard sounds like from the men who have to call it.

Florida scored 118 and allowed 24 at home. Auburn does not play those two games, and the rest of the card is why that matters. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999. Ole Miss, Missouri, South Carolina, Texas in Austin, and Georgia in Atlanta on Halloween are still waiting. Two home blowouts do not change those. The honest range for this roster is still 7-5 or 8-4. First 3-0 since 2019 is what Saturday would be. That is the stake. Not a history lesson. The first night the season can stop being easy.

They are 2-0 because home never got close. Saturday is the first night that can.

## Takeaways

- Florida is 2-0 because two home games never got close. That is why the record looks like a start. It is also why the record is not a test.
- The Campbell flags did not cost them at 45-3. The same flags, and the same slow start, can cost them at Auburn if the game is still close.
- Sumrall's uneasiness is the story this week. Auburn is the first real test of the standard.
- Stop the run or you have no chance. Philo has been enough at home. Saturday is the first time the crowd is not his.
- Honest range is still 7-5 or 8-4. Saturday would be the first 3-0 since 2019. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999.
