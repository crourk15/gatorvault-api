# Home Was the Easy Part

By GatorVault Staff · Sep 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Jon Sumrall walked off a 52–3 win unhappy. The Gators cannot beat the Gators — not if Saturday is Auburn.

Florida is 2–0. Florida Atlantic and Campbell are in the books, both at home, both never close. The building wanted to party. Sumrall did not. Ten minutes after Campbell, he said he was happy his guys got the win and then told the truth: a lot he was not pleased with. If Florida plays the second half the way it played the second half Saturday, he said, they are about to have a real rude awakening. Walk into conference play like that, he said, and they will get absolutely embarrassed.

That is the story two games in. Not the scoreboard. The standard.

The first key is the one he kept repeating. The Gators cannot beat the Gators. Every time they felt an explosive in the second half, another flag came out. First-and-25 in this league, he said, and you might as well get ready to give the ball back. T.J. Shanahan Jr. got flagged on back-to-back snaps — a hold that wiped a Baugh run that was already gone, then a personal foul. Roderick Kearney picked up another. Seven flags after the break. One in the first half. Three in the whole opener. The second half is the problem, and Auburn will not let them play through it.

The second key is maturity. Sumrall emptied the bench to get people snaps, and some of those snaps told on people. Second-team and third-team guys got a chance to prove people right or wrong. Some of them, he said, proved why they are not playing. The standard is a Florida Gator standard, first team or third, and it is not negotiable. Freshmen have been here since January. Readiness is not a speech. It is whether you can take the snap when your name is called. A few guys found out Saturday they are not there yet.

Defense has a different key, and it has been the same for two weeks. Start faster. After FAU, Sumrall watched the tape and said the issue was situational awareness and football IQ — third-and-short with a corner sitting nine yards off, tempo they did not handle, coverage that got cautious when it needed to get sticky. They limited explosives. They did not manage the pace. After Campbell he said the same thing in a new way: the first drive got them, a couple of completions down the field, then they settled in. He told the DBs not to get scared after a catch. At corner you are going to get beat. Next play. They held Campbell to a field goal on that opening series and nothing after that. Better. Still not a three-and-out to start a game. They have not had one yet. Saturday they will need it.

Aaron Philo is the clean part of the tape, and Sumrall has said so twice. After FAU he said the interception was not on Philo — catchable ball, should have been a completion — and that Philo operated, communicated at the line, and led. After Campbell he said Philo played efficiently, operated well, and they are playing good football with him in the game. That is the key. The numbers sit behind it: 16 of 21 both Saturdays, 517 yards, five scores, one pick. The room is still open. Tramell Jones Jr. is still here. Philo has the starts because the operation has held.

Jadan Baugh is the other clean part. Sumrall likes the physical run and the long one. Last year Baugh's longest was 23 yards. He has now hit a long one in back-to-back weeks, including the 75-yarder against Campbell. That is the key. The box follows: 296 yards and five scores in two games, 14 carries each Saturday. What Sumrall did not like is everything around him. The depth of the backfield was subpar — a guy going the wrong way, a missed protection. Up front, the first group was not awful and is not where it needs to be. They did not give Baugh as many lanes as they should have. After FAU he had been fired up about perimeter blocking from the receivers and tight ends. After Campbell the line was the flags. Lovett, Harris, Moore, Shanahan, Ugorji still own the names he said out loud before Week 1. Continuity was the ask in August. Clean football is the ask now.

Special teams is the third side, and Sumrall treats it like it. Vernell Brown III was sluggish early with everybody else, then he took over the night — two punt-return touchdowns, a receiving score, the kind of play Sumrall said raises your chance to win if you do not screw it up. Dallas Wilson's Campbell night sat right next to it: five catches, 104 yards, the 40-yard finish. Sumrall called Wilson a high-ceiling player and said he is going to keep getting better. Complementary football is the word he used — offense, defense, and the kicking game taking the momentum back together. Florida had that in the first half. They lost it when the flags started.

Zoom out and the slate has not changed. Auburn, then Ole Miss, Missouri, South Carolina, Texas in Austin, Georgia in Atlanta on Halloween. Two home blowouts do not move those games. The honest range is still 7–5 or 8–4 if the road weeks look like football. 2–0 is what you are supposed to be after FAU and Campbell. The live question is the one Sumrall asked: can they get better in five days?

Auburn is not the downhill team people still picture. This is no-huddle shotgun and Byrum Brown, the quarterback who followed the staff. He wants the first read and the keep. Against Baylor he threw short and let Keshaun Singleton, Jeremiah Koger, and Chas Nimrod run after the catch. When they got to him late, he threw three interceptions. When they knocked him off the first read, they sacked him five times. The front sat on the run and still gave up 333 through the air. We have the official picture. We have not sat the broadcast.

Florida wins there if they do what Sumrall is actually asking, then handle the opponent. Do not beat yourselves. If Auburn loads the box for Baugh, Philo has to throw over it and finish the drive. If Brown has time, he will take the short throw and the keep — get to him before the first read, stay home when he pulls it. Singleton, Koger, and Nimrod already turned catch-and-run into a Baylor problem. Get there and tackle them. Start faster on defense. Keep the flags in the bag.

Sumrall said he is a little uneasy with where they are. You can win games the way they have won these two. If they are not urgent and detailed this week, he said, Saturday will not be what they desire. That is the whole piece. The first two nights were at home and never close. The next one is Auburn, and the head coach already told you what has to change.

## Key takeaways

- Sumrall's first key: the Gators cannot beat the Gators. Second-half flags killed explosives (seven after the break; one in the first half; three in the whole opener).
- Maturity and readiness: some depth snaps proved why those guys are not playing. He said conference play will embarrass them if that does not change.
- Defense has to start faster. Situational awareness was the FAU tape. Campbell they settled in after the first drive. Still no three-and-out to open a game.
- Philo is operating (Sumrall: efficient, communicates, leads). Baugh hits the long run. The line and the backfield depth are not where he wants them.
- To win at Auburn: clean football first, then throw over a loaded box, crowd Byrum Brown, and tackle Singleton, Koger, and Nimrod after the catch.
