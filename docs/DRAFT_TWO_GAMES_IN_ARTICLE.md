# Home Was the Easy Part

By GatorVault Staff · Sep 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Florida is 2-0. That is what you are supposed to be after Florida Atlantic and Campbell. Both were at home. Neither game got close. That is why the record looks like a start. It is also why the record is not a test. Home was the easy part. Saturday at Auburn is the first night Jon Sumrall's standard has to leave the Swamp.

They opened against Florida Atlantic and put 66 on the board. Aaron Philo went 16-of-21 for 275 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception. Jadan Baugh ran 14 times for 160 yards and three scores. Vernell Brown III caught six for 117 and a touchdown. Eric Singleton Jr. hurt his ankle and did not finish. The night never asked Florida a hard question.

Sumrall still watched the tape like it had. The next Monday was football IQ: knowing what the play is asking, the down and distance, when they speed it up, when to crowd a receiver and when to give him space. On 3rd-and-short a corner sat nine yards off the ball. That is too far. They kept FAU from the long passes. They let FAU speed the game up. The interception was a catchable ball that should have been a completion, not on Philo. Philo changed the play at the line when he needed to, and led. The win was real. The tape already had the things that can lose you a Saturday when the other team can punch back.

Campbell was supposed to be the second easy one. For a while it sounded like the first drive of a different game. Completions down the field. A field goal. Sumrall told the defensive backs not to get scared after a catch. At corner you are going to get beat. Next play. Florida still had to come get the lead.

They did. Philo scored from the 3. Patrick Durkin hit a 28-yard field goal. After one it was 10-3, and then the Swamp got the night it came for. Vernell Brown III scored twice on punt returns, 64 and 70. Dallas Wilson had the 40-yard touchdown. Brown scored again as a receiver, 14 yards. Baugh finished it from the 2 with 20 seconds left. Halftime was 45-3. Philo went 16-of-21 for 242 yards and two scores, the same clean night in a different week.

That is when the scoreboard stopped telling the truth. Sumrall put the backups in and the flags came with them. Every time they hit a big play after halftime, another one came out. T.J. Shanahan Jr. on back-to-back snaps: a hold that wiped a Baugh run that was already a big gain, then a personal foul. Roderick Kearney picked up another. That is 1st-and-25. In this league the drive is already over. The Gators cannot beat the Gators. Seven flags after halftime. One in the first half. Three in the whole opener. Up 45-3 those flags do not change the score. If they show up when the game is still close, they are how you lose. That is the first thing about this team that Auburn can actually use.

The night still had one more run in it. Late in the third Baugh went 75. Last year his longest was 26 yards. In two weeks he has 296 yards and five scores on 14 carries each Saturday. Campbell stayed on three. The Swamp left with 52-3 and a 2-0 start it had not had in five years.

Fans walked out with the number. Sumrall walked to the locker room unhappy, and that is the part that matters this week. Happy they got the win. Not pleased with a lot of the rest. Second-team and third-team guys got a chance to prove people right or wrong, and some of them proved why they are not playing. Sumrall called the depth behind Baugh subpar. A guy went the wrong way on one run. Somebody missed a protection call. The starting offensive line was not awful and is not where it needs to be. They did not give Baugh as many rushing lanes as they should have. Year One under Sumrall is a standard before it is a celebration. Freshmen have been here since January. The standard is not negotiable. Play that second half in conference and they are about to have a real rude awakening. Walk in like that and they will get absolutely embarrassed. He is not sour after 52-3. He is a first-year coach who knows the next Saturday is the first one that can embarrass him.

Monday, Sumrall was still a little uneasy. Better than Saturday, and not gone. When they rolled the depth of the roster into the game, they did not play clean football. He wants them to finish on the quarterback. He wants sacks. These games count twice, he said. Overall record and conference record. The last two weeks counted once. If he has to get anybody ready to play SEC football, they have the wrong people here.

That urgency is the culture. Auburn is the first place it gets measured. The spacing and the shots get everybody's attention. What kills you is the run game. If you do not stop the run against them, you have no chance. Jeremiah Cobb and Omar Mabson II are both over 5.5 yards a carry and have 265 combined. Byrum Brown ran for a thousand yards and 14 scores last year. He is physical, playing with confidence, a lot of time in this system.

Eric Singleton Jr. and Emmanuel Oyebadejo are out of the boot. Both were very limited Monday. Both are questionable. Sumrall said there is a shot. He is not ready to say they will play.

Philo is confident and not easily rattled. If he plays like he has played through the first part of this year, Sumrall said, he will give them a chance to win every week. At home that has been enough. Saturday it has to be enough with a crowd that is not theirs.

Tuesday is when the coordinators have to say how that travels. Buster Faulkner has to take Philo on the road for the first time. DJ Durkin's defense plays with its hair on fire, plays fast, plays confident. Same preparation every week, no matter who they play. You are not just playing 11 defenders. You have the crowd. Faulkner said Philo is cool, calm, and collected. They will coach him through it.

Brad White has to take a defense that just spent two weeks in the Swamp and put it in a fight. Auburn's offense already knows the system. A lot of those guys have been in it with Alex Golesh for years. Pace, tempo, skill, a nasty offensive line. Fist fight. Rock fight. Be ready for that.

Florida scored 118 and allowed 24 at home. Auburn does not play those two games. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999. Ole Miss, Missouri, South Carolina, Texas in Austin, and Georgia in Atlanta on Halloween are still on the card. Two home blowouts do not change those. The honest range for this roster is still 7-5 or 8-4. First 3-0 since 2019 is what Saturday would be.

The easy part already happened. It happened in the Swamp, twice, against teams that never got close. Saturday is the first night the flags can cost them, the first night the start can cost them, the first night the standard has to hold while the game is still a game. Home was the easy part. Auburn is the first Saturday that can tell you whether the rest of it is real.

## Takeaways

- Florida is 2-0 because two home games never got close. That is why the record looks like a start. It is also why the record is not a test.
- The Campbell flags did not cost them at 45-3. The same flags, and the same slow start, can cost them at Auburn if the game is still close.
- Sumrall's standard is the story this week. Auburn is the first place it has to travel.
- Stop the run or you have no chance. Philo gives them a chance to win every week if he is who he has been at home.
- Honest range is still 7-5 or 8-4. Saturday would be the first 3-0 since 2019. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999.
