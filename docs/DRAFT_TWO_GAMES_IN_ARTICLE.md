# Home Was the Easy Part

By GatorVault Staff · Sep 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Florida is 2-0. That is what you are supposed to be after Florida Atlantic and Campbell. Both were at home. Neither game got close. That is why the record looks like a start, and why it is not a test. Home was the easy part. Saturday at Auburn is the first night that has to leave the Swamp.

They put 66 on Florida Atlantic because Aaron Philo went 16-of-21 for 275 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception, and Jadan Baugh ran 14 times for 160 and three, on a night that never had a chance to get close. Vernell Brown III caught six for 117. Eric Singleton Jr. left on an ankle. A building will take 66-21 and call itself ready, which is why Sumrall spent the next Monday on the snaps that would not survive a building that is not theirs: football IQ, knowing what the play is asking, the down and distance, when they speed it up, when to crowd a receiver and when to give him space. On 3rd-and-short a corner sat nine yards off the ball, which is too far. They took away the long passes and let FAU speed the game up. The interception was a catchable ball, not on Philo. Philo changed the play at the line when he needed to, and led. The win was real. The tape was the first sign that 2-0 could still be a disguise.

Campbell was the next home Saturday, and the first series made that sign public, completions down the field and a field goal, the kind of start Sumrall told the defensive backs not to get scared of because at corner you are going to get beat and the next play is the one that matters. If that opening lives at Auburn while the score is still a score, home has already run out. What followed was the problem hiding inside a blowout. Baugh scored from the 2 and it was 45-3, with Vernell Brown III already having taken the night, Dallas Wilson the 40, and Philo 242 yards and two scores, which is what happens when the other team cannot stay close and still cannot tell you Saturday.

Because it was 45-3, that night could afford what Saturday cannot. Sumrall put the backups in and the flags came with them, T.J. Shanahan Jr. on back-to-back snaps, a hold that wiped a Baugh run that was already a big gain, then a personal foul, then Roderick Kearney, which is how you get to 1st-and-25 and how a drive dies in this league. Seven after halftime, one in the first half, three in the whole opener. Up 45-3 those flags do not change the score. If they show up when the game is still close, they are how you lose, and Byrum Brown and Jeremiah Cobb are the ones who get that version if Saturday stays a game.

That is the part of 2-0 that does not travel, and Sumrall was already carrying it toward Auburn before the night was even finished. Late in the third Baugh went 75, leftover from a game that had already been decided, the only kind of night that can absorb seven flags. Last year his longest was 26 yards. In two weeks he has 296 yards and five scores on 14 carries each Saturday. Campbell never got off three, and the Swamp walked out with 52-3 and a 2-0 start it had not had in five years.

Sumrall walked to the locker room unhappy with that number in his pocket, happy they got the win and not pleased with a lot of the rest, because second-team and third-team guys got a chance and some of them showed why they are not playing. Sumrall called the depth behind Baugh subpar. A guy went the wrong way on one run. Somebody missed a protection call. The starting offensive line was not awful and is not where it needs to be. They did not give Baugh as many rushing lanes as they should have. Year One in this building is a coach deciding, in public, that 52-3 is not the point. Freshmen have been here since January. Play that second half in conference and they are about to have a real rude awakening. Walk in like that and they will get absolutely embarrassed, and the team that can embarrass them is already in Jordan-Hare, where Jeremiah Cobb and Omar Mabson II are both over 5.5 yards a carry with 265 combined and Byrum Brown ran for a thousand yards and 14 scores last year, physical, playing with confidence, a lot of time in this system.

Monday, that was still in the rooms. It had cooled, and it had not left. When they rolled the depth of the roster into the game they did not play clean football, and Sumrall wants them to finish on the quarterback and get sacks because these games count twice, overall record and conference record, and the last two weeks counted once. If he has to get anybody ready to play SEC football, they have the wrong people here. The spacing and the shots get everybody's attention. What kills you is the run. If you do not stop it, you have no chance. Home let Florida pick the terms, and Auburn is not built to hand them back, including the chance they walk in without Singleton and Oyebadejo, who came out of the boot for a limited Monday and are questionable, which is as far as Sumrall would go.

Philo is the piece that has not had to prove anything away from here. He is confident and not easily rattled. If he plays like he has played through the first part of this year, Sumrall said, he will give them a chance to win every week. That has been enough with this crowd behind him. It has to be enough in a building that will not help him. If the close game Sumrall is already carrying is real, Tuesday is when Faulkner and White have to call it. Buster Faulkner has to take a quarterback who has only started at home and keep him clean when DJ Durkin's defense is playing with its hair on fire, fast and confident. Same preparation every week, no matter who they play. You are not just playing 11 defenders. You have the crowd. Faulkner said Philo is cool, calm, and collected, and they will coach him through it, while Brad White has to take a defense that just spent two weeks in the Swamp and put it in a fight that already knows Alex Golesh's offense, pace and tempo and skill and a nasty offensive line, the fist fight and rock fight he told them to be ready for.

Florida scored 118 and allowed 24 at home. Auburn does not play those two games, and the card after that is why a close one this week is not just Week 3. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999. Ole Miss, Missouri, South Carolina, Texas in Austin, and Georgia in Atlanta on Halloween are still waiting. Two home blowouts do not change those. The honest range for this roster is still 7-5 or 8-4. First 3-0 since 2019 is what Saturday would be.

Home was the easy part. Saturday can spend 2-0, and the rest of the card is waiting to keep it.

## Takeaways

- Florida is 2-0 because two home games never got close. Saturday is the first night that can decide whether that was a start.
- The Campbell flags did not cost them at 45-3. The same flags, and the same slow start, can cost them at Auburn if the game is still close.
- Sumrall walked off 52-3 already carrying Auburn. The flaws and the run game are the same problem.
- Stop the run or you have no chance. Philo has been enough at home. Saturday is the first time the crowd is not his.
- Honest range is still 7-5 or 8-4. Saturday would be the first 3-0 since 2019. Florida has not won at Auburn since 1999.
