# Home Was the Easy Part

By GatorVault Staff · Sep 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Jon Sumrall walked off a 52–3 win unhappy. The Gators cannot beat the Gators — not if Saturday is Auburn.

Florida is 2–0. Florida Atlantic and Campbell are in the books, both at home, both never close. The building wanted to party. Sumrall did not. Ten minutes after Campbell, he said he was happy his guys got the win and then told the truth: a lot he was not pleased with. If Florida plays the second half the way it played the second half Saturday, he said, they are about to have a real rude awakening. Walk into conference play like that, he said, and they will get absolutely embarrassed.

That is the story two games in. Not the scoreboard. The standard.

He kept coming back to the same thing. The Gators cannot beat the Gators. Every time they felt a big play in the second half, another flag came out. First-and-25 — that is a holding or a personal foul shoving you so far backward that the drive is basically over — and in this league, he said, you might as well get ready to give the ball back. T.J. Shanahan Jr. got flagged on back-to-back snaps. First a hold that wiped a Baugh run that was already gone. Then a personal foul. Roderick Kearney picked up another. Seven flags after the break. One in the first half. Three in the whole opener. The second half is the problem, and Auburn will not let them play through it.

Sumrall emptied the bench to get people snaps, and some of those snaps told on people. Second-team and third-team guys got a chance to prove people right or wrong. Some of them, he said, proved why they are not playing. The standard is a Florida Gator standard, first team or third, and it is not negotiable. Freshmen have been here since January. Readiness is not a speech. It is whether you can take the snap when your name is called. A few guys found out Saturday they are not there yet.

Defense has been the same conversation for two weeks: start faster. After FAU, Sumrall watched the tape and said the issue was football IQ on the grass — knowing the down and distance, knowing when the other team is speeding the game up, knowing when to crowd a receiver and when to give him a little space. Third-and-short, and a corner was sitting nine yards off the ball. They did not handle Campbell's pace. They did keep FAU from hitting the long ones. After Campbell he said it another way. The first drive got them — a couple of completions down the field — then they settled in. He told the defensive backs not to get scared after a catch. At corner you are going to get beat. Next play. They held Campbell to a field goal on that opening series and nothing after that. Better. Still not a three-and-out to start a game. They have not had one yet. Saturday they will need it.

Aaron Philo is the clean part of the tape, and Sumrall has said so twice. After FAU he said the interception was not on Philo — catchable ball, should have been a completion — and that Philo ran the offense, talked at the line, and led. After Campbell he said Philo played efficiently and they are playing good football with him in the game. That is why he still has the starts. The numbers sit behind it: 16 of 21 both Saturdays, 517 yards, five scores, one pick. The room is still open. Tramell Jones Jr. is still here.

Jadan Baugh is the other clean part. Sumrall likes the physical run and the long one. Last year Baugh's longest was 23 yards. He has now hit a long one in back-to-back weeks, including the 75-yarder against Campbell. The box follows: 296 yards and five scores in two games, 14 carries each Saturday. What Sumrall did not like is everything around him. The other running backs were sloppy — a guy going the wrong way, a missed block in protection. Up front, the first group was not awful and is not where it needs to be. They did not open as many running lanes for Baugh as they should have. After FAU he had been fired up about the receivers and tight ends blocking on the edge. After Campbell the line was the flags. Bryce Lovett, Knijeah Harris, Harrison Moore, T.J. Shanahan Jr., and Emeka Ugorji still own the spots he named before Week 1. August was about keeping the same five together. This week is about playing clean.

Special teams is the third side, and Sumrall treats it like it. Vernell Brown III was sluggish early with everybody else, then he took over the night — two punt-return touchdowns, a receiving score, the kind of play Sumrall said raises your chance to win if you do not screw it up. Dallas Wilson's Campbell night sat right next to it: five catches, 104 yards, the 40-yard finish. Sumrall called Wilson a high-ceiling player and said he is going to keep getting better. Offense, defense, and the kicking game have to hand the momentum back and forth. Florida had that in the first half. They lost it when the flags started.

The rest of the schedule has not changed. Auburn, then Ole Miss, Missouri, South Carolina, Texas in Austin, Georgia in Atlanta on Halloween. Two home blowouts do not move those games. The honest range is still 7–5 or 8–4 if the road weeks look like football. 2–0 is what you are supposed to be after FAU and Campbell. The question Sumrall is asking is whether they can get better in five days.

Auburn is not the downhill run team people still picture. This offense is no-huddle shotgun — they line up fast, almost never under center — with Byrum Brown at quarterback, the transfer who followed the staff. Brown wants to see his first option right now, the first receiver or the keep where he pulls the ball and runs it himself. Against Baylor he threw those short throws and let Keshaun Singleton, Jeremiah Koger, and Chas Nimrod catch it and run. When Baylor got to him late, he forced the ball and threw three interceptions. When they got in his face before he could find that first option, they sacked him five times.

Auburn's defense is a different picture. Their front — the defensive line and the linebackers stacked behind it — came downhill to stop the run. Baylor only got 103 yards on the ground. That is the trade. When you load extra bodies near the line to take the run away, you leave space behind them. Baylor threw for 333 yards into that space. We have the official picture. We have not sat the broadcast.

Florida wins there if they stop beating themselves, then handle what Auburn actually is. If Auburn does the same thing to Baugh — extra hats near the line to take the run away — Philo has to throw over those extra hats and finish the drive. If Brown has time, he will take the short throw or pull it and run. Get to him before he finds that first option, and stay home when he keeps it. Singleton, Koger, and Nimrod already turned those short catches into long runs against Baylor. Get there and tackle them. Start the game on defense. Keep the flags in the bag.

Sumrall said he is a little uneasy with where they are. You can win games the way they have won these two. If they are not urgent and detailed this week, he said, Saturday will not be what they desire. The first two nights were at home and never close. The next one is Auburn, and the head coach already told you what has to change.

## Key takeaways

- The Gators cannot beat the Gators. Second-half flags killed the big plays (seven after the break; one in the first half; three in the whole opener).
- Some depth snaps proved why those guys are not playing. Sumrall said conference play will embarrass them if that does not change.
- Defense has to start faster. After FAU it was down-and-distance and tempo. After Campbell they settled in after the first drive. Still no three-and-out to open a game.
- Philo is running the offense (efficient, talks at the line, leads). Baugh hits the long run. The line and the other backs are not where Sumrall wants them.
- At Auburn: play clean first. If they load extra bodies to stop Baugh, throw over them. Get to Byrum Brown early. Tackle Singleton, Koger, and Nimrod after the catch.
